# White River town-scale utility model (3 km by 3 km, Pierce County, Washington)

Plates 56 to 65, `outputs/56_*` to `outputs/65_*`. Run, in order: `python scripts/build_town.py` (tiled detection), `python scripts/build_town_model.py` (chains, spans, supports, check against the map), `python scripts/build_town_extras.py all`, `python scripts/plot_town.py`.
Lidar: 3DEP `WA_PierceCounty_1_2020`, flown 2020-04-13, about 25 returns per m2, NAVD88. 270 million points read across 36 tiles of 500 m (40 m buffer) covering 9 km2 centred on the White River substation. Overture Maps power features are used only to check the results and as training labels; they are incomplete and sometimes wrong.

## Why this site
Navasota, Texas has 2.3 returns per m2 in its 3DEP flight, too sparse for wires. Of the Pierce County towns sampled, Bonney Lake, Eatonville and Orting (nine windows each) reached a median 20 to 25 per m2 and Sumner, Buckley and Puyallup 11 to 14 (one window each); the White River area matches the best, has a transmission corridor and a large substation, and the earlier 800 m study here supplies the method and thresholds.

## Method
1. **Tiles.** Each 500 m tile is read with a 40 m buffer; height above ground, wire points (linear, near-horizontal, open-space neighbourhoods), pole columns and elevated non-mass returns are computed per tile, and only what lies inside the tile is kept, so merging never duplicates.
2. **Chains and anchors.** Wire points are joined across tile edges; anchors are the detected poles, the ends of wire chains and the supports from sag-curve peaks.
3. **Spans.** A parabola is fitted to the returns in a corridor between neighbouring anchors; spans with at least half observed and 25 returns are kept (thresholds from the 800 m study's shifted-data test, not tuned to the map). Missing stretches of a kept span are the fitted curve, and 21% of the wire length is such a fill.
4. **Extensions in this stage:** a diagnosis of where mapped lines have no wire, a learned support detector, equipment detection and a learned score for candidate spans with straight-line bridging of dangling ends.

## Results
- **Model:** 352 wire chains, 77 detected poles, 1,755 fitted wires and 108 km of wire in the first model (79% of the wire length observed, the rest interpolated), 148 supports. False wire length in four shifted-data runs is 6.7% of the real length. Hiding 20 m and 40 m stretches of well-observed wire: the fitted curve is off by 4 and 5 cm rms, a straight line by 8 and 24 cm (plate 65).
- **Against the map (first model):** 99.8% of fitted wire lies within 10 m of a mapped line, but only 28% of the 38 km of mapped high-voltage line has a fitted wire within 6 m.
- **Why mapped lines are missed (plates 58, 59).** 41% of the mapped length has elevated returns on it but no fitted wire, because the supports at its ends were not found (the median distance from a mapped line point to the nearest detected support is 62 m); 15% has no elevated returns at all on open ground, which points at a mapped line that is absent or misplaced; 7% is under vegetation; 9% has a wire 6 to 15 m away.
- **Learned support detector (plates 60, 61).** 25,836 column-shaped candidates (from 457,328), 205 labelled positive by mapped poles within 4 m, a gradient-boosted tree model scored on whole 500 m tiles it did not train on: area under the ROC curve 0.94. At score 0.12 it keeps 190 detections, 21% within 3 m of a mapped pole (27% within 5 m) and 14% (19%) of mapped poles found. The geometric supports reach 22% precision and 12% recall at 3 m, so the learned detector is **not an improvement**. Only 41% of mapped poles have any column-shaped return within 3 m (59% within 5 m), which caps recall for any detector at this density. No deep-learning library or GPU is available here, so this is gradient-boosted trees, not a neural network.
- **Equipment (plates 62, 63).** In the main yard 433 compact structures are found; 72% of the 97 mapped switches, transformers and portals have one within 3 m, against 29% for random points in the yard (46% against 9% within 1.5 m). Pole-mounted equipment: a thickening rule fires at 16 of 148 supports (11%) against 8% for control columns. That is weak, and the map has no pole-mounted equipment (all 97 mapped items are in the yards), so those candidates are **unverified**; naming one a transformer needs imagery.
- **Joining the network (plates 64, 65).** Candidate spans between 664 anchors (125 from the learned detector) are scored by a tree model on held-out tiles (area under the curve 0.81, folds 0.61 to 0.93). At score 0.60 it adds 246 spans, 12.4 km, 81% of it within 6 m of a mapped line (the first rule: 90%). 7 line ends facing each other were bridged (738 m, 81% within 6 m of a mapped line). Mapped line with a wire within 6 m: 33% with the first rule, 38% with the model's spans, 39% with the bridges. Supports linked by spans form 44, then 16, then 12 separate pieces, the largest holding 20%, 36% and 57% of the supports.

## Limits
- The map is the only reference. It misses poles (about 7 per km of line, where spacing of 40 to 60 m gives 17 to 25), places some lines wrongly, and has no pole-mounted equipment, so precision figures are floors and recall figures are measured against a partial list. No field survey, GNSS or imagery review of the detections was done.
- Recovery of the mapped network is still about 39%. The largest single cause is missing supports (41% of the mapped length), which better pole detection at this density has not fixed; imagery of pole shadows or a higher-density flight would be the next step.
- Spans added by the model and bridged line ends are partly or wholly inferred; heights of bridged wires come from a sag coefficient fitted to the observed spans. Their precision is measured against the same map the model was trained on (on tiles held out from training).
- The model has not been tested at another site. The imagery-textured 3D model is not built yet, as planned.
