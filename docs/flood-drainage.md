# Drainage, culvert obstructions and flood-stage road vulnerability: Ghent and the Hague, Norfolk, Virginia

Run `python scripts/build_flood.py` then `python scripts/plot_flood.py`. Plates 51 to 55; GeoTIFFs in `outputs/flood/`; tables in `data/processed/flood/` (`stats.json`, `culvert_candidates.csv`, `road_segments.csv`).
Lidar: 3DEP `USGS_LPC_VA_Norfolk_2013_LAS_2015`, flown 2013-04-01, 6.6 returns per m2 (2.0 reach the ground or road surface), NAVD88. A 1.4 km window at 36.868 N, 76.301 W.

## Method
1. **Bare earth at 1 m** from ground returns (class 2) plus road-surface returns (class 11) within 1.5 m above and 1 m below a ground-only terrain (taller class-11 returns are bridge decks and ramps; 57% of road returns are kept). Gaps (36% of cells, under buildings and trees) are filled by smooth interpolation.
2. **Hydrological conditioning with pysheds:** fill pits and depressions, resolve flats, D8 flow directions, flow accumulation. (WhiteboxTools could not be downloaded here.) Closed depressions come from the difference between the filled and original terrain.
3. **Culvert candidates:** a depression whose spill point is at a road and which the embankment holds back by at least 10 cm; a least-cost path to lower ground beyond the road gives the cut needed, and the terrain is re-conditioned with the cut to see the storage released.
4. **Bathtub flooding** at stages 1.5 to 4.0 m (NAVD88): terrain below the stage connected to the open water seen in the lidar (voids with no returns), or any terrain below the stage (ponding).
5. **Which roads can flood.** Overture drivable segments. A road is not assumed to be elevated because something is above it: each segment is classified from the map tags (bridge, tunnel, level) **and** from what the lidar sees along its centreline. *Bridge*: tagged, with a deck seen overhead or no road surface seen at ground level; it is judged at the deck height, so water at ground level below it does not count. *Tunnel*: tagged, with the road surface not seen; not assessed. *At grade, open cut*: tagged tunnel but the surface is seen and nothing is overhead (a sunken road, which does flood). *At grade*: everything else, including streets under trees and roofs. A road is impassable where 30 cm or more of water stands on it (15 cm difficult), a scenario threshold.

## Results
- **Terrain:** held-out ground returns matched to 4.5 cm rms, road-surface returns 13 cm, 95% of all within 10 cm.
- **Drainage:** 75 closed depressions (60 m2 or more, 15 cm or deeper) hold 67,000 m3 over 11 ha; the deepest (7 m) are underpass and tunnel-approach trenches, which drain by pumps in reality.
- **Culvert candidates:** 10 depressions are held back by at least 10 cm of road embankment (23,000 m3); the strongest barrier is 0.74 m at Hampton Boulevard. Several have only 10 to 20 cm of barrier, which is within a few times the terrain error and is weak evidence. Opening them releases almost no stored water in the model because most spill into the same depressions.
- **Flooding (connected to open water, ha):** 2.8 at 1.5 m; 6.6 at 2.0; 27 at 2.5; 122 at 3.0; 181 at 3.5; 192 at 4.0 (of 196 ha). Between 2.5 and 3 m the whole low plain fills.
- **Roads (51 km drivable; 48.1 km at grade, 1.7 km open cut, 0.3 km at grade but unobserved, 0.5 km bridge, 0.4 km tunnel):** impassable with 30 cm of water, ponding scenario / connected scenario: 2.5 m: 25 / 13 km; 3 m: 39 / 38 km; 4 m: 50 / 50 km. With every depth shifted by the 13 cm lidar road-surface error the ponding figure is 22 to 30 km at 2.5 m and 36 to 43 km at 3 m. The largest connected piece of the network holds 50% of its length at 2.5 m (connected scenario), 12% at 3 m and under 1% at 4 m.
- **What changed after the accuracy review.** The first version treated any segment with a return more than 3 m above its centreline as an elevated road and never flooded it. The lidar shows that rule also removed tree-lined streets and roofs overhanging service roads: 7.9 km of ordinary streets (169 untagged segments had something overhead, almost all tree canopy). Of the 4 segments tagged bridge, 2 have a deck seen above them (median deck 6.8 m) and are kept as bridges; 1 tag conflicts with the lidar and is treated as at grade. Five segments tagged tunnel are open cuts whose road surface is seen at ground level, with nothing over them, and are now flooded like any other road.

## Accuracy against independent data
All numbers are in `data/processed/validation/flood.json` (run `python scripts/validate_flood.py`).
- **Overlapping flight lines, same ground.** The 2013 survey was flown in three overlapping swaths. On flat unbuilt ground their 4 m cell means agree to a robust spread of 3.0 cm on roads and 3.8 cm on open ground; 88 to 90% of cells agree within 10 cm and 98% within 20 cm (2,305 road cells, 2,691 open-ground cells). A swath difference is about 1.4 times a single swath's error.
- **USGS's own DTM of the same survey** (a second processing of the same points, not a second survey): ours minus theirs has bias -3 cm, robust spread 4.1 cm, rms 9 cm over 610,000 unbuilt cells, and 3.7 cm robust spread on road cells. So gridding and ground classification are not the main error.
- **Chesapeake Conservancy 1 m land cover** (2013-14 imagery plus lidar): 90% of the cells along our two bridge segments are roads, and 24% of the cells along at-grade segments are "tree canopy over roads", confirming that overhead returns on ordinary streets are mostly trees. Along segments classed as tunnel, 57% are roads and 9% tree-over-road.
- **Water voids.** The no-return voids used as the source water (0.15% of the window) do not coincide with Chesapeake's water class (0.02%): intersection over union 0. JRC surface water shows 0% occurrence at both. So these voids are not mapped open water; the connected scenario is a scenario about where water enters, not a measured tidal connection.
- **Overture bridges.** One of the two bridge segments lies inside a mapped bridge footprint; the tags and the footprints come from the same OpenStreetMap source, so this is consistency, not independent evidence.
- **Not possible here.** The 2011 FEMA survey over the same ground holds no points inside the window. USGS's height-above-ground raster reads higher than our nDSM everywhere (median 7.4 against 2.6 m), so it uses a different definition and is not used to judge the classification. No tide gauge, benchmark or observed flood extent was compared: the absolute vertical accuracy and the flood depths are unchecked against real events.

## Tests
- **Planted embankments** (V valley, 10 m road embankment, a culvert in half the trials, noise at the measured terrain level): found 67% of the time at 0.5 m, 75% at 1 m, 88% at 2 m, 13% at 0.3 m, 0% at 0.2 m or less; never flagged when the culvert was open.
- **Vertical error:** a 10 cm error in the stage changes the connected flooded area at 3 m from 108 to 134 ha, and at 2.5 m from 24 to 53 ha; 20 cm is larger still. On a plain this flat the datum matters more than the model.
- **Cell size:** 1 m and 2 m cells give the same flooded area within 1%.
- **Buildings and trees:** about half the connected flooded area at 3 m and above lies under building and tree surfaces, which are filled terrain; the plates show terrain depth everywhere.

## Limits
- **Not a flood forecast.** Static bathtub: no rain, drains, pumps or flow; the stage is a water-surface height in NAVD88, with no tide or storm assigned. The source water is the set of no-return voids in the south-west of the window (ponds and basins), not a mapped tidal creek, and water arriving from outside the window is not modelled.
- Culverts and storm drains are not in the lidar or the map data, so "candidates" are places where terrain suggests an obstruction, not confirmed culverts; real culverts open below the surface.
- Lidar vertical accuracy against benchmarks or tide gauges was not checked (internal consistency, flight-line agreement and USGS's own DTM only), and the 2013 terrain predates later construction.
- Overture road geometry is OpenStreetMap-derived; bridge and tunnel flags come from it and are checked against the lidar, as described above.
